Build one vocabulary entry for a Korean learner of English, based on the learner's own input below.
The entry feeds four quiz types: English↔Korean matching, fill-in-the-blank with multiple choice, fill-in-the-blank typing, and Korean→English typing.
</task>

<input>
<term>${term}</term>

<rules>
<identity>
- term: copy the input term exactly, only trimming spaces and lowercasing it unless it is a proper noun or an acronym. Do not correct it to a different word. If the term is misspelled so badly that you cannot identify it, still copy it and set every other string field to "".
- pos: one of noun, verb, adjective, adverb, phrasal_verb, idiom, preposition, conjunction, other.
  - Multi-word verb + particle (give up, look after, run out of) → phrasal_verb.
  - Fixed multi-word expression that is not a phrasal verb (once in a blue moon, by the way) → idiom.
  - If the term has several parts of speech, pick the one that matches user_meaning_ko; if user_meaning_ko is empty, pick the most frequent one.
- cefr: the CEFR level (A1, A2, B1, B2, C1, C2) of this sense.
</identity>

<meaning>
- If user_meaning_ko is not empty: the entry MUST describe that sense. meaning_ko = user_meaning_ko, only trimmed and with obvious typos fixed. Never replace it with a different sense, even if another sense is more common (e.g. bank + "둑" → the river-bank sense, not 은행).
- If user_meaning_ko is empty: meaning_ko = the single most representative Korean gloss of the most frequent sense.
- Style of meaning_ko: at most 20 characters, no English, no parentheses, no lists separated by commas.
  - verb / phrasal_verb → Korean dictionary form ending in -다 (포기하다, 버리다)
  - adjective → Korean adnominal form (꺼리는, 중요한)
  - adverb → adverb form (간신히, 드물게)
  - noun → noun (결과, 둑)
- disambiguation_ko: if a Korean learner who sees only meaning_ko could reasonably write a different common English word, write a short Korean note (max 40 characters) that points to THIS word's nuance, e.g. "강·호수 가장자리의 비탈 (은행 아님)". Otherwise "".
- near_miss_synonyms: 0–4 other common English words or phrases (base form) a learner might write for meaning_ko. Never include the term itself or any of its inflected forms. [] if none.
</meaning>

<forms>
- forms has all eight keys. Fill only the keys that apply to the pos; every other key is "".
  - noun: base (singular), plural. Uncountable-only nouns → plural "".
  - verb, phrasal_verb: base, past, past_participle, present_participle, third_person. For phrasal verbs inflect the verb and keep the particle(s): gave up, given up, giving up, gives up.
  - adjective: base, comparative, superlative (more reluctant / most reluctant when periphrastic). Non-gradable adjectives → comparative "" and superlative "".
  - adverb, idiom, preposition, conjunction, other: base only.
- forms.base must equal term.
- Use standard American spelling in forms. Put a British spelling of the base in term_variants, not in forms.
- term_variants: other accepted spellings of the base term only (colour, e-mail, well known → well-known). Never synonyms. [] if none.
</forms>

<example>
- If user_example is not empty AND it is a grammatical, natural English sentence AND it contains the term (in any inflected form, contiguous) used in the sense of meaning_ko → example = user_example exactly as written (keep its punctuation and capitalization).
- Otherwise write a new example that follows ALL of these:
  1. 8–16 words, one sentence, ends with . ! or ?
  2. Everyday, concrete situation. No names of real people, brands, politics, religion, violence, or sexual content.
  3. Uses the term exactly once, in the sense of meaning_ko. No other form of the term appears anywhere else in the sentence.
  4. The term is NOT the first word of the sentence.
  5. The word directly before the term is NOT "a" or "an" (for singular countable nouns use the/my/this/his… or choose a plural or other construction), so the article cannot reveal the answer.
  6. Context makes this term the most natural fit, but the sentence must not define the word, paraphrase it, or contain one of its synonyms.
  7. Every other word in the sentence is at or below the term's CEFR level.
  8. Phrasal verbs and idioms appear as one contiguous span (He gave up smoking — not He gave it up).
  9. Prefer an inflected form (past, plural, -ing, third person, comparative) when it is natural; the base form is fine when the grammar needs it.
- example_ko: natural Korean translation of the example that matches it exactly in meaning. It should express the target naturally with meaning_ko or its conjugation where Korean grammar allows.
</example>

<cloze>
- cloze_answer: the exact span of the example that is the term (with its inflection), copied character for character. For phrasal verbs and idioms, the whole contiguous span.
- cloze_sentence: the example with exactly that one span replaced by <>. Nothing else changes (spacing, punctuation, capitalization).
- Check: replacing <> in cloze_sentence with cloze_answer must give back example exactly.
- cloze_form: which forms key cloze_answer equals (compare case-insensitively): base, past, past_participle, present_participle, third_person, plural, comparative, superlative. If past and past_participle are identical, choose the one that matches the grammar of the sentence. Use "other" only when cloze_answer matches no forms value.
- cloze_accepted: other spellings of cloze_answer that must also be marked correct when typed (British spelling, accepted hyphenation, e.g. "travelled" for "traveled"). Never synonyms or other forms. [] if none.
</cloze>
</rules>
